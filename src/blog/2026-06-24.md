I've had experience with manga (Japanese comics) localization recently. Almost every building block can be handled by AI. As a supervisor, you just guide the process and correct parts that aren't good enough.

Why "almost" every building block? Image reconstruction isn't a defeated beast. I tried a lot of tools, and only one was good enough to clean up certain cases - it's Google Pixel's Magic Eraser.

Here is the creation workflow:

- Find the original raw content.

- Extract and translate the text.

- Improve image quality, fix compression artifacts, adjust sharpness, and apply other tweaks.

- Clean and reconstruct the image over original text.

- Place and format the new text.

- Check the result and iterate on previous steps to get a perfect outcome.

- Publish the result.

I found it useful to use GIMP with plugins for creative work, Topaz Gigapixel for improving the imagery, Google Sheets to track progress and keep the text handy, and Gemini with Gems for text processing (OCR extraction, grammar checking, and translation with options for native phrasing and tone).

I also tried fully automated tools. They don't produce the same quality as doing it manually, but specific, targeted tools are gorgeous and save a ton of time. A lot of AI stuff is a scam - there is always one winner, and the others just make things worse.

I think the process will become more automated, but only a human can decide how the final result should look for another human in a creative field.
