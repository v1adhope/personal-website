I've used Sway, Hyprland, and AeroSpace. They're tiling window managers, an alternative way to organize windows. Hyprland is the best Linux Wayland compositor, with a large ecosystem of native tools (hypridle, hyprlock, hyprpaper, xdph), cool animations, and no bugs in my experience. AeroSpace is an alternative for macOS with a few bugs and poor UX. The value of these tools is that you can focus on the main task. A window fills the available screen space or shares it with others, usually taking half when there are two. Forget about moving windows or resizing one, then the other. They resize automatically. Workspaces are customizable, and each application can have its own place.

I think humans went the wrong way, as with keyboards and their typewriter legacy. How many times have you wondered why the keys are staggered or why only the space bar sits under the most powerful finger?
