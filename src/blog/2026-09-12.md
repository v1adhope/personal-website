I’ve finally stopped writing code by hand. As a software engineer, I basically don't write code manually anymore, except for specific areas or low-level stuff like OS configs. I've been experimenting with new tools for a while now, figuring out what works for me and what doesn't.

General takeaways:

- Listen to and watch the frontier labs and their engineers. They also run blogs with genuinely brilliant insights.

- Favor tools built by your model's provider. They're tuned for that provider's models and usually ship more features.

- Nothing gets merged without a human eye on the code. You still have to make sure the output is good enough.

- Cheap, on one condition: don't burn tokens carelessly.

- The only real limit is how many ways you can put your own mind to use. Every day you find a new idea for applying it to life.

- Don't fight new tech. Adapt your workflow to it instead.

OpenCode Go $10:

- The usage limit is too low for coding work, plus the models feel weaker. They don't have that "artificial soul", that style of communicating with the user.

Claude Pro sub $25:

- Sonnet 5 on high is fine for throwaway or burner work.

- Opus 5 on high is fine when you already know the output you want.

- xhigh effort gives the best results, but you rarely get to use it, since on Sonnet and up the session runs out before you finish.

All of this holds for one feature at a time. Spread it across several tasks at once and, in most cases, the limits run dry before you clear the load.

I put all of this into practice on a small project, manga-theka: <https://github.com/v1adhope/manga-theka>
