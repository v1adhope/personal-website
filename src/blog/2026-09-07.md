I don't use my phone the way a normal person does. The closest I've seen anyone else describe it is Ed Sheeran:

"I haven't had a phone since 2015. I got an iPad. I moved everything onto email which I reply to once a week."

That's basically me. If I need to read, send a message, watch something, whatever, I'd rather open the iPad than wreck my eyes on the phone and carry it around just to get interrupted by it.

The one thing that still ties me to a phone is the SIM card. Which gets me thinking: we've got crypto for money, so why are we all still politely renting our phone numbers from carriers, which is to say governments?

They're bad by design. Every country makes you hold a local number to get online, open a bank account, or sign up for anything. Each one also sets its own rules for how long a SIM stays alive: miss a week of use, or skip a single one-cent top-up, and they reclaim or block it.

It's a clean surveillance tool, and a clean way to lock out anyone without a number from an "allowed" country, though someone determined enough to get around it never actually gets stopped. All it really does is make life uncomfortable for everyone else and create a parasitic niche that exists to break the gate, like account resellers.

So these days my phone's only job is keeping my SIM cards warm so they don't get blocked.

Mobile internet barely works where I live right now, so I've turned it off entirely. No service, no money from me. At this point the expensive phone itself is dead weight. Anyone want to buy my Pixel? (Half joking, only half.)
