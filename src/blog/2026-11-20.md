An email about a browser project somehow turned into me building my own website.

The update came from [Ladybird](https://ladybird.org/), an open-source browser with its own engine built from scratch. Curious about how they're building an alternative to Chromium, I opened their GitHub organization to look around.

There I found the [source for their website](https://github.com/LadybirdBrowser/ladybird.org). How does it work? What do they use? Those questions led me to [Astro](https://astro.build/), then to a tutorial on building a personal blog in Astro's documentation. Following that tutorial turned a small investigation into a project of my own.

The site's structure was clear in my mind. Figuring out how it should look was harder.

That meant getting acquainted with the designer's side of the job: learning Figma, exploring design systems, and practicing building components. Along the way, I studied Material UI and its approach to [accessibility](https://mui.com/material-ui/getting-started/accessibility/).

Useful things to understand as an engineer. Having an idea of what a page needs doesn't mean you know how to arrange it, make it readable, or make its interactions clear.

Working through those choices gave me more appreciation for how much time design takes. Research, try something, look at it again, change it. Repeat. And that's on my own little site. When a design change has significant financial consequences, each decision needs to be weighed carefully.

After 29 design iterations in my Codex design studio, I had a mockup ready to build. Then came scaffolding the project, adjusting the implementation, and stress-testing the result with Chromium MCP. Moving from a mockup to a working page brought another round of changes.

Years ago, my posts lived on Medium. Then came LinkedIn. Now I have [my own website](https://blog.vladislavgardner.workers.dev/). Maybe you're already reading this there.

The goal was a place where I could decide how my writing lives and what belongs there. Somewhere to share things beyond my professional work, too.

A little corner of the web that feels like mine. Funny that it started with reading someone else's newsletter.
